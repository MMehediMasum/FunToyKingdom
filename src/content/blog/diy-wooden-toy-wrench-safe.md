## Choosing The Right Wood

Picking the right wood is key for a safe DIY wooden toy wrench. Wood affects the toy's look, feel, and safety. Choose wood that is strong but easy to work with.

Some woods are better for toys because they do not splinter or break easily. It is important to select wood that will last and keep kids safe.

### Best Wood Types For Toys

Hardwoods are often the best choice for wooden toys. They are strong and smooth. Softwoods can work but may dent or splinter easier.

* Maple: Very strong and has a smooth finish.
* Beech: Hard and fine-grained, good for detailed work.
* Ash: Tough and flexible, resists cracking.
* Birch: Light color and strong, easy to shape.
* Cherry: Durable and ages well with use.

### Safety And Durability Considerations

Wood for toys must be free from toxins and splinters. Avoid woods with strong smells or that may cause allergies. Sand edges smooth to prevent cuts.

Durable wood helps the toy last longer. Check that wood is dry and not cracked. Finish the wood with non-toxic paint or oil to protect it.

* Use untreated, natural wood to avoid chemicals.
* Sand all surfaces until smooth.
* Seal wood with child-safe finishes.
* Choose wood that resists cracking and breaking.
* Check wood for knots or weak spots.

## Cutting And Shaping Wood Pieces

Careful cutting and shaping also prevent sharp edges and splinters. This makes the toy safer for children to play with.

### Safe Cutting Techniques

Use sharp tools to make clean cuts. Dull blades can cause rough edges or slips. Always cut slowly and steadily for control.

Wear safety glasses and keep fingers away from the blade. Secure the wood firmly to avoid movement. Choose the right saw or knife for the wood thickness.

* Use clamps to hold wood in place
* Cut on a stable surface
* Follow marked lines carefully
* Keep fingers clear of the blade path

### Sanding For Smooth Edges

Sanding removes splinters and smooths rough edges. It makes the wooden wrench safe and pleasant to hold. Start with coarse sandpaper and finish with fine grit.

Sand all edges and flat surfaces evenly. Check for sharp spots and sand again if needed. Use a sanding block to keep even pressure.

* Begin with 80-grit sandpaper
* Move to 120-grit for smoother finish
* Finish with 220-grit for soft edges
* Wipe dust off before assembling
