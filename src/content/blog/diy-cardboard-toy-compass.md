## Materials Needed

Making a cardboard toy compass is a fun and simple project. You only need a few easy-to-find materials. These materials help build a compass that works and looks cool.

Gather all the items before you start. This will make the project go smoothly and save time.

### Cardboard

Cardboard is the main material for your compass. It is strong but easy to cut and shape. Use a piece that is flat and clean.

Old boxes or packaging work well. Choose cardboard about the thickness of a cereal box.

### Compass Needle

You need a small, lightweight needle to act as the compass needle. A sewing needle or a straight pin works best.

The needle should be sharp enough to balance and spin freely on the compass base.

### Magnet

A magnet is necessary to magnetize the needle. Use a small bar magnet or a fridge magnet strip.

Rubbing the needle with a magnet will make it point north when placed on the compass.

### Pin Or Thumbtack

A pin or thumbtack is used to fix the needle to the cardboard base. It must allow the needle to spin freely.

Choose a sharp pin with a small head that can hold the needle without blocking movement.

### Paper Or Cardstock

Paper or cardstock helps mark the compass directions. You can draw or print the north, south, east, and west labels.

Cardstock is thicker and lasts longer, but regular paper works fine for a simple compass.

### Glue Or Tape

Use glue or tape to attach parts of the compass together. White glue or double-sided tape works well.

Make sure the glue dries clear and does not block the needle’s movement.

### Scissors Or Craft Knife

Scissors or a craft knife will help you cut the cardboard and paper pieces. Be careful and ask for adult help if needed.

A craft knife gives more precise cuts but requires a cutting mat to protect surfaces.

### Marker Or Pen

A marker or pen is useful to draw compass markings and decorate the cardboard. Use a dark color for clear visibility.

You can add arrows and letters to show directions on the compass face.

Cutting the cardboard base is the first important step in making your toy compass. You need a flat, sturdy piece to support the compass parts.

Before cutting, collect all tools. This makes the process faster and safer. Use a ruler for straight lines and a sharp blade for clean cuts.

* Metal ruler or straight edge
* Pencil or pen to mark lines
* Cutting mat or thick cardboard underlay

## Making The Compass Needle

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=the-compass-needle-is-the-key-part-that-shows-direction.-you-can-make-it-easily-with-cardboard-and-a-magnet.&w=424&h=324&c=7)

The compass needle is the key part that shows direction. You can make it easily with cardboard and a magnet.

This guide will help you create a simple needle for your DIY cardboard toy compass.

### Cutting The Needle Shape

Start by cutting a thin, pointed shape from a piece of cardboard. The needle should be about 2 to 3 inches long.

Make sure both ends are sharp, but one end should be slightly sharper to show the north direction.

### Magnetizing The Needle

You need to make the needle magnetic to point north. Use a small magnet and rub it on the needle.

Rub the magnet in one direction about 30 times on the needle to magnetize it well.

### Attaching The Needle To The Compass Base

Place a small pin or a straight pin through the center of the needle. This will let it spin freely.

Fix the pin on the center of the cardboard base using a small piece of clay or cork underneath to hold it steady.

### Balancing The Needle

Check if the needle spins smoothly and balances on the pin. Adjust the cardboard or pin if it tilts.

A well-balanced needle will move easily and point to the magnetic north.

## Calibrating Your Compass

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=calibrating-your-cardboard-toy-compass-helps-it-point-the-right-way.-it-makes-your-compass-more-accurate-and-fun-to-use.&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Calibrating your cardboard toy compass helps it point the right way. It makes your compass more accurate and fun to use.

Follow simple steps to adjust your compass. This ensures it shows true directions when you play or explore.

### Find A Flat Surface

Place your cardboard compass on a flat, steady surface. This stops the needle from moving too much and helps it settle correctly.

Choose a spot away from metal objects or electronic devices. These can affect the compass needle and give wrong readings.

### Align The Compass Needle

Gently spin the needle until it stops moving. The needle points to the magnetic north. Make sure it is free to move without touching the cardboard.

If the needle sticks, check if your compass parts are loose or bent. Fix them before calibrating again.

### Adjust For Magnetic Declination

Magnetic declination is the angle difference between magnetic north and true north. It varies by location. Adjust your compass needle accordingly.

* Find your local declination online or on a map
* Turn the needle slightly to match this angle
* This helps your compass show directions more accurately

### Test Your Compass

Check your compass by comparing it with a real compass or a map. Make sure both point in the same direction.

Repeat calibration if the directions do not match. Small adjustments improve your compass’s accuracy.

## Tips For Accurate Navigation

Making a DIY cardboard toy compass is fun and educational. It helps you learn how to find directions.

To use your compass well, follow some simple tips for accurate navigation. These tips will guide you step by step.

### Keep The Compass Flat

Hold the cardboard compass flat and steady. This helps the needle move freely and point north correctly.

If the compass tilts, the needle may stick or give a wrong direction. Place it on a flat surface if needed.

### Avoid Metal Objects Nearby

Metal objects can change the direction of the compass needle. Keep your compass away from keys, phones, or magnets.

Use the compass in an open space to get a true reading of the earth’s magnetic field.

### Use The Sun For Checking

The sun rises in the east and sets in the west. Use it to check if your compass points correctly.

* In the morning, the sun is in the east.
* At noon, the sun is south if you are in the northern hemisphere.
* In the evening, the sun is in the west.

### Mark Your Directions Clearly

Write or draw the main directions on your cardboard compass. Label north, south, east, and west clearly.

This helps you quickly read the compass and understand the direction you are heading.

### Practice Regularly

Use your cardboard compass often to get better at navigation. Practice in different places and times of day.

Frequent use helps you trust your compass and improves your direction skills.
