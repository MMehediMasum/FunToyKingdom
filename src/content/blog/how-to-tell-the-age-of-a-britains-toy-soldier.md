## History Of Britains Toy Soldiers

Britains toy soldiers are small figurines made to look like real soldiers. They have been popular with collectors and children for many years. Learning their history helps to tell how old a toy soldier is.

The history of Britains toy soldiers shows how they changed over time. These changes make it easier to date each figure.

### Origins And Early Models

Britains started making toy soldiers in 1893. The company was founded by William Britain. Early models were made of lead and hand-painted with care.

These first figures were simple and small. They showed soldiers from different armies and times. The early models are often called "hollow cast" because of how they were made.

### Evolution Over The Decades

Over the years, Britains toy soldiers changed in shape and material. In the 1930s, the company used plastic for some figures. This made the toys lighter and safer.

After World War II, designs became more detailed. Paint colors and uniforms matched real soldiers closely. This helped collectors identify the time period of each figure.

* 1890s: Lead hollow cast soldiers
* 1930s: Introduction of plastic figures
* 1940s-50s: More detailed painting and shapes
* Later years: Plastic became the main material

### Key Manufacturing Milestones

Several important steps in making Britains soldiers help date them. For example, early figures have a stamp on the base with "W. Britain". Later figures used "Britains Ltd".

Changes in packaging, size, and weight also mark different periods. Collectors look for these clues to find the soldier’s age. Each change marks a new chapter in the toy’s story.

* 1893-1920s: Lead figures with "W. Britain" stamp
* 1930s: Start of plastic figures
* 1940s-50s: More precise painting and molds
* 1960s: Shift to all plastic production
* Stamping changed to "Britains Ltd" after 1920s




## Materials And Construction

Britains toy soldiers show many signs of their age in the materials and construction used. Knowing these clues helps collectors understand their history.

The changes in materials and building methods happened over many years. Each era has unique features in the toy soldiers.

### Types Of Materials Used

Early Britains soldiers were made from lead. Later, the company switched to other metals and plastics. Each material type points to a certain time period.

* **Lead:**Used before World War II. Heavy and soft.
* **Alloys:**Mixtures of metals like tin and antimony. Used mid-1900s.
* **Plastic:**Started in the 1950s. Lighter and more durable.

### Casting Techniques

How the soldiers were cast helps date them. Early soldiers were hand-poured, while later ones used machines.

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Technique** | **Period** | **Characteristics** |
| Hand Casting | Before 1930 | Irregular edges, small air bubbles |
| Machine Casting | 1930 to 1950 | Smoother surfaces, consistent shape |
| Injection Molding | Post 1950 | Plastic parts, precise details |

### Paint And Detailing Styles

Painting styles changed as well. Early soldiers have hand-painted details, while newer ones use stencils or decals.

* **Hand Painting:**Uneven brush strokes, slight color differences.
* **Stencil Painting:**Clean lines, repeated patterns.
* **Decals and Transfers:**Sharp images, used from the 1960s.

## Identifying Markings And Stamps

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=britains-toy-soldiers-have-unique-markings-and-stamps-that-help-identify-their-age.-collectors-use-these-marks-to-date-the-figures-accurately.&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Britains toy soldiers have unique markings and stamps that help identify their age. Collectors use these marks to date the figures accurately.

Understanding these markings can give insight into the history and value of the toy soldier. This guide explains common marks, date codes, and changes over time.

### Common Manufacturer Marks

Britains often stamped their toy soldiers with the company name or logo. These marks show which factory made the piece.

* The name "Britains" or "Britains Ltd" appears on many figures.
* Some soldiers have the country of origin, like "Made in England."
* Early figures may show the "Britains Ltd London" stamp.
* Later toys sometimes include a small crown or trademark symbol.

### Date Codes And Serial Numbers

Many Britains figures have date codes or serial numbers. These help pinpoint the year of production.

| Marking Type | What It Means | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Date Code | Shows the production year or batch | "45" for 1945 or "67" for 1967 |
| Serial Number | Identifies the mold or design | "No. 102" for a specific soldier type |
| Batch Number | Used to track production runs | "Batch 3" |

### Variations In Markings Over Time

Britains changed their markings as the company grew and technology advanced. Knowing these changes helps date a figure.

Here are some key changes over time:

* Early 1900s: Simple "Britains" stamp, sometimes hand-etched.
* 1930s-1950s: More detailed marks with "Britains Ltd" and location.
* 1960s onward: Plastic figures have molded marks, less ink stamping.
* Post-1980: Modern trademarks and logos replaced older stamps.

## Design Features By Era

Britains toy soldiers have changed a lot since they were first made. You can tell their age by looking at how they were designed.

Different times had different styles for uniforms, bases, and colors. These details help collectors know when a toy soldier was made.

### Uniform Styles And Accessories

Early Britains soldiers wore detailed uniforms with many small parts. Later versions had simpler styles to save costs.

Accessories like hats, belts, and weapons changed with the era. Some soldiers have removable parts, others do not.

* Pre-1920s: Very detailed, hand-painted accessories
* 1920s-1950s: More uniform shapes, some molded accessories
* 1960s and later: Plastic parts, fewer details on uniforms

### Base Shapes And Sizes

Older soldiers often stand on oval or round metal bases. Newer ones have plastic bases in many shapes.

Size also varies. Early soldiers were smaller, about 54mm tall. Later models grew larger or changed scale.

* Pre-1930s: Small metal bases, mostly oval or round
* 1930s-1960s: Larger metal bases, some square shapes
* After 1960s: Plastic bases, many shapes and sizes

### Color Schemes Through The Years

Early soldiers have rich, hand-painted colors with small details. Later ones use fewer colors and simpler paint jobs.

The paint style helps date the soldier. Bright colors faded over time, and some color schemes match certain periods.

| Era | Color Style | Details |

* Rust spots may form on older metal parts.

### Restoration Effects On Value
