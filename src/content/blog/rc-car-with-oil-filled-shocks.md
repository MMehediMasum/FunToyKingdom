## Benefits Of Oil Filled Shocks

Oil filled shocks are a popular upgrade for RC cars. They help improve the car’s performance on rough surfaces.

These shocks use oil to absorb bumps better than regular springs. This makes driving smoother and more controlled.

### Enhanced Shock Absorption

Oil filled shocks absorb impacts from bumps and jumps well. The oil inside slows down the shock’s movement.

This slows the car’s bounce and keeps it steady on uneven ground. It helps protect the car’s parts from damage.

### Improved Stability

The oil inside the shocks controls how fast they move. This control helps keep the RC car stable during turns and fast runs.

With less bouncing and better grip, the car stays on track. This makes it easier to handle and more fun to drive.

### Longer Component Life

Oil filled shocks reduce wear on the car’s parts. They soften the hits that damage the suspension and chassis.

This means less frequent repairs and longer use of the car. The shocks keep the RC car running smoothly for a long time.

## How Oil Filled Shocks Work

### Shock Structure And Components

Oil filled shocks have several key parts that work together. These parts control how the shock moves and absorbs energy.

* **Shock Body:**The main tube that holds the oil and parts.
* **Piston:**A small piece inside the shock that moves up and down.
* **Oil:**The fluid inside that slows the piston’s movement.
* **Spring:**Surrounds the shock body and helps return it to position.
* **Seals:**Keep the oil from leaking out of the shock.

### Oil Damping Mechanism

The piston moves inside the shock body and pushes the oil through small holes. This slows down the piston.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Part** | **Function** |
| Oil | Creates resistance to piston movement |
| Piston Holes | Control oil flow speed |
| Shock Body | Holds oil and piston |
| Spring | Returns shock to original position |

## Installation Tips

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=installing-oil-filled-shocks-on-your-rc-car-improves-handling-and-control.-follow-these-tips-for-a-smooth-setup.&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Installing oil filled shocks on your RC car improves handling and control. Follow these tips for a smooth setup.

Take your time and prepare all needed tools before starting the installation process.

### Tools Needed

* Small Phillips screwdriver
* Needle-nose pliers
* Shock oil
* Shock shaft cleaner or paper towels
* Thread locker (optional)
* Small container for shock oil

### Step-by-step Installation

1. Remove the old shocks by unscrewing the mounting screws carefully.
2. Clean the shock shafts with a soft cloth to remove dirt and oil.
3. Fill the new shocks with the correct amount of oil using a syringe or dropper.
4. Attach the shock ends to the car’s suspension arms and chassis mounts.
5. Tighten all screws securely but avoid over-tightening to prevent damage.
6. Test the suspension movement to ensure smooth operation and proper damping.

### Common Mistakes To Avoid

| Mistake | Effect | Tip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Using wrong shock oil viscosity | Poor suspension response | Match oil thickness to car specs |
| Not cleaning shock shafts | Oil leaks and damage | Wipe shafts before filling shocks |
| Over-tightening screws | Stripped threads or broken parts | Tighten screws just enough |
| Air bubbles in shocks | Inconsistent damping | Fill shocks slowly to avoid bubbles |

## Maintenance For Optimal Performance

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=proper-care-keeps-your-rc-car-running-smoothly.-oil-filled-shocks-need-regular-attention.&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Proper care keeps your RC car running smoothly. Oil filled shocks need regular attention. This helps maintain control and prolongs the shock life.

Follow simple maintenance steps. These improve performance and reduce costly repairs. Focus on inspection, oil replacement, and fixing issues quickly.

### Regular Inspection

Check your shocks often for leaks or damage. Look for cracks or worn seals. Also, test if the shocks compress and rebound smoothly.

* Inspect shock body for cracks or dents
* Check for oil leaks around seals
* Test shock movement for smooth action
* Look for loose mounting screws

### Oil Replacement

Change the oil in your shocks regularly. Old oil can thicken or get dirty. This affects shock performance and handling.

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Oil Type** | **Viscosity** | **Use** |
| Silicone Oil | 30-50 wt | General use for smooth action |
| Silicone Oil | 70-90 wt | For stiffer, more controlled shocks |
| Mineral Oil | Varies | Less common, may damage seals |

### Troubleshooting Issues

If your shocks feel too soft or too hard, check the oil level. Strange noises can mean air inside or damaged parts. Fix these to keep control.

* Soft shocks: add thicker oil or check for leaks
* Hard shocks: use thinner oil or check for dirt inside
* Noises: bleed air out by pumping the shocks slowly
* Replace damaged seals or springs promptly
