## Classic Tic Tac Toe Rules

Tic Tac Toe is a simple two-player game. Players take turns marking spaces on a 3x3 grid.

The goal is to place three of your marks in a row. The first to do so wins the game.

### Basic Gameplay

Players choose either X or O as their mark. X usually goes first. Each player picks an empty square to place their mark.

The game ends when one player forms a line of three marks or when all squares are filled.

### Winning Patterns

* Horizontal line: three marks across any row
* Vertical line: three marks down any column
* Diagonal line: three marks across either diagonal

Any of these lines wins the game immediately.

### Common Strategies

| Strategy | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Center Control | Take the center square to maximize winning chances. |
| Block Opponent | Place your mark to stop the opponent from winning. |
| Fork | Create two possible winning moves at once. |
| Force Move | Make the opponent respond to avoid losing. |

## Ultimate Tic Tac Toe

Ultimate Tic Tac Toe is a complex twist on the classic Tic Tac Toe. It adds layers of strategy and challenge to the simple game.

The game uses multiple boards and rules that change how players take turns and win.

### Board Layout

The game board consists of nine smaller Tic Tac Toe boards arranged in a 3x3 grid. Each small board is called a "local board."

* Each local board has nine spaces for moves.
* Players try to win local boards to claim spots on the big board.
* The big board itself is also a 3x3 grid made up of these local boards.

### Turn Mechanics

Each turn, a player marks one empty space on a local board. The local board chosen depends on the previous move.

If the previous player played in a space, the next player must play in the local board matching that space's position.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Previous Move Position** | **Next Local Board to Play** |
| Top-left | Top-left local board |
| Center | Center local board |
| Bottom-right | Bottom-right local board |
| If the local board is full or won | Player can choose any local board |

### Winning The Game

Players win local boards by making three in a row on that small board. The goal is to win three local boards in a row on the big board.

* Win three local boards horizontally, vertically, or diagonally on the big board.
* If all local boards are won or full without a big board winner, the game is a draw.
* Claiming more local boards increases chances to win the big board.




## Misère Tic Tac Toe

Misère Tic Tac Toe is a twist on the classic game. Players try to avoid making three in a row.

This variation changes how you think about winning and losing. It makes the game more tricky and fun.

### Rule Changes

In Misère Tic Tac Toe, the main rule is reversed. Making three in a row means you lose, not win.

* Players alternate placing X or O on the board.
* If a player creates three in a row, they lose immediately.
* The game ends when the board is full or a player loses.

### Winning By Losing

The goal is to force your opponent to make three in a row first. You win by making them lose.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Outcome | Result |
| Player makes three in a row | Player loses |
| Opponent makes three in a row | Opponent loses |
| Board fills with no three in a row | Game is a draw |

### Tactical Moves

Players must plan moves carefully. Some common tactics help avoid losing.

* Block spots that create three in a row for you.
* Force your opponent into making bad moves.
* Use empty spaces to delay losing patterns.
* Think ahead to avoid traps.

## Quantum Tic Tac Toe

Quantum Tic Tac Toe is a new way to play the classic Tic Tac Toe game. It uses ideas from quantum physics to change how players make moves.

This version adds a twist that makes the game more complex and fun. It helps players think in new ways.

### Quantum Concepts

Quantum Tic Tac Toe uses two main ideas from quantum physics: superposition and entanglement. These ideas change how moves are made and how the game ends.

Superposition means a move can be in many places at once until it is confirmed. Entanglement links moves so one affects the other.

* **Superposition:**Moves can be in more than one square at the same time.
* **Entanglement:**Moves are connected and affect each other.
* **Collapse:**When a move is finalized, the superposition collapses into one square.

### Gameplay Differences

In Quantum Tic Tac Toe, players place "quantum" moves that link two squares. These moves stay uncertain until triggered to collapse.

The game ends when the board collapses to a regular Tic Tac Toe state. Players need to watch for linked moves and the collapse effects.

* Players mark two squares per move instead of one.
* Moves remain uncertain and connected until collapse.
* Collapses happen when loops or conflicts occur.
* Winning depends on the final collapsed board.

### Tips For Success

Think ahead and track all possible move outcomes. Watch how moves are linked and where collapse might happen.

Try to create entangled moves that force your opponent into tough choices. Stay flexible and ready to change plans after collapse.

* Keep track of all quantum links on the board.
* Plan moves that create loops to cause collapse.
* Force your opponent into uncertain positions.
* Adapt quickly after moves collapse into real squares.
