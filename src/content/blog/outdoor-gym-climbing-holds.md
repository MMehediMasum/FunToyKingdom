## Types Of Climbing Holds For Outdoor Gyms

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=outdoor-gyms-use-different-climbing-holds-to-create-fun-and-challenging-routes.-these-holds-help-climbers-practice-different-grips-and-techniques.&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Outdoor gyms use different climbing holds to create fun and challenging routes. These holds help climbers practice different grips and techniques.

Choosing the right type of hold is important for safety and skill building. Here are common types of climbing holds found in outdoor gyms.

### Jugs And Slopers

Jugs are large holds that are easy to grab with the whole hand. They provide a strong and comfortable grip for beginners and warm-ups.

Slopers are rounded holds with no edges. Climbers must use open-hand strength and balance to hold on to them.

* **Jugs:**Good for beginners and rest points
* **Slopers:**Require palm pressure and body positioning

### Crimps And Pinches

Crimps are small edges that let climbers grip with their fingertips. They build finger strength and precision.

Pinches are holds that you squeeze from both sides. They challenge hand and thumb strength.

* **Crimps:**Small edges for fingertip grips
* **Pinches:**Hold by squeezing between fingers and thumb

### Edges And Pockets

Edges are flat holds with a clear rim. Climbers use their fingertips or whole hand depending on size.

Pockets are holes or openings in the wall. Climbers put one or more fingers inside to hold on.

* **Edges:**Flat holds with a defined lip
* **Pockets:**Finger holes with varying depths

## Materials And Durability

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=outdoor-gym-climbing-holds-need-to-be-strong-and-last-long.-choosing-the-right-material-helps-them-resist-wear-and-tear.&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Outdoor gym climbing holds need to be strong and last long. Choosing the right material helps them resist wear and tear. Durability is key for safety and cost-effectiveness.

This guide covers popular materials and how they stand up to weather. It also shares tips to keep climbing holds in good condition.

### Resin And Polyurethane Holds

Resin holds are heavy and solid. They give good grip but can crack if hit hard. Polyurethane holds are lighter and more flexible. They resist chipping better than resin.

Both materials are popular for outdoor use. Polyurethane is often preferred for its balance of strength and weight. Resin holds may need more care to avoid damage.

### Weather Resistance

Outdoor holds face sun, rain, cold, and heat. Good weather resistance stops fading, cracking, and warping. Polyurethane holds resist UV rays and water well. Resin holds can weaken in strong sunlight.

* Polyurethane holds keep color longer
* Resin holds may need UV protectant coatings
* Water can cause resin to crack over time
* Freezing and thawing cycles stress both materials

## Safety Tips For Outdoor Climbing

Outdoor climbing can be an exciting and rewarding experience, but it also comes with inherent risks. To minimize these risks and ensure a safe climbing experience, it's essential to follow proper safety tips and guidelines.

### Proper Gear And Footwear

Proper gear and footwear are crucial for a safe climbing experience. Climbers should invest in high-quality climbing shoes, harnesses, and ropes that are designed for outdoor climbing.

* Climbing shoes should fit snugly and provide support for the feet
* Harnesses should be adjustable and comfortable to wear
* Ropes should be made of durable, weather-resistant materials

### Spotting And Supervision

Spotting and supervision are essential for a safe climbing experience, especially for beginners. Climbers should always climb with a partner or spotter who can provide assistance and guidance in case of an emergency.

Spotters should be trained in basic climbing safety and rescue techniques, and should be aware of the climber's skill level and experience.

### Regular Inspection

Regular inspection of climbing equipment and gear is crucial for ensuring a safe climbing experience. Climbers should inspect their gear regularly for signs of wear and tear, and replace it as needed.

* Climbing ropes should be inspected for signs of wear, such as fraying or cuts
* Harnesses should be inspected for signs of wear, such as broken buckles or torn webbing
* Climbing shoes should be inspected for signs of wear, such as worn-out soles or broken straps
